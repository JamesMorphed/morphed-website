Morphed is the studio I wanted to hire when I was on the client side. No account manager filtering the brief. No junior designer guessing your intent. Just one practitioner with thirteen years in the trade, moving from strategy to sketch to shipped artefact without a handoff in sight.

My career has been the long version of a generalist. Five years as a B2B designer at April Six during its Agency of the Year run. Three years as Senior Art Director at PS London across education, automotive, and consumer. Three years at IGT leading a global design team that shipped the Best Lottery Product of the Year. Then Nokia, where I led UX for their Brand Hub through a global rebrand. A year of full-time freelance through Pfizer's Immersive Studio.

Today I'm Head of UI/UX at Light & Wonder, leading design across their iGaming division — a PAM platform used by operators globally and player behaviour products across the slot portfolio. The studio is the archive of everything before that, and the shingle for select work outside the day job.

06 — How I work

Three principles. Same ones for 13 years.

The rules I return to when the brief is ambiguous and the deadline is tight.

01 / Simplicity

Distil, don't decorate.

Most briefs are more complex than they need to be. The first job is finding the simplest version of the problem that's still worth solving — before a single pixel hits a canvas.

02 / Sketchbook first

Paper before pixels.

Every project starts with a pen and a cheap sketchbook. Software is good at making bad ideas look convincing. Paper isn't. Paper is where the thinking actually happens.

03 / Technologist

Tools earn their keep.

Figma, Webflow, Relume, Spline, Vev, AI. None of them matter on their own — but the right combination collapses a month of work into a week. That's the competitive edge.
